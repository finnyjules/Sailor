Shader takes

A throwaway spike. Six requests with four first-draft takes each. Claude wrote them standing in for the model, under the rules a real feature would enforce: the standard preamble, three to five dials, and loops with fixed bounds. Every take runs through Sailor’s own ShaderFxRenderer and is checked automatically. Nothing was retouched before this render.

Breakage is not the problem

All 24 compiled first try and none costs noticeable GPU time. The repair loop and cost limits still belong in the product (a smaller model will break more), but they aren’t the hard part.

Taste is the problem

11 of 24 takes are keepers. The automatic checks passed all five misses (muddy, too dark, or missing the image). Only a human pick, or a model looking at the render, catches those.

So four takes earns its keep

Every request produced at least one keeper, but you couldn’t tell in advance which take it would be. Showing four and letting you pick beats one result plus rewording.

Create held up as well as transform

The two requests with no starting shader did as well as the transforms, or better (ink: four out of four). What predicted success was a concrete, physical prompt, not having a base shader.
